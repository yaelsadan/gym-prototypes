Citizen Café — Mobile v01 · primary design surface

The mobile Café playground, and from here the primary surface for Café design exploration. Desktop (Current/cafe-playground-desktop/) is frozen as the initial scaffold and reference. Café is a sibling of Gym, not a new visual system: the phone shell, statusbar, transition shell, A/V check, live video stack, participant labels, room clock, glass dock, Leave & report and milky bottom sheets are the existing Gym mobile components at their existing values, taken from student-practice-rooms-mobile and student-transition-screens-mobile. Shared primitives live in css/gym-mobile.css and js/gym-mobile.js; everything Café-specific is in css/cafe-mobile.css and js/cafe-mobile-states.js. This pass is product hierarchy and behaviour, not polish.

What is reused, not redrawn

  • Live Café is the Practice Room shell. Vertical stacked video split, cream Assistant participant labels in their fixed per-tile positions, the flat top-right room clock with its yellow final-seconds treatment, the floating milky-glass dock, the mic-off flag, the camera-off cream face, and Leave & report. No new video-call layout was designed.
  • A/V check is the canonical mobile A/V block — the same card, preview, 52px glass Camera / Mic / Blur controls, live mic echo rings and permission helpers. Device choice and an optional playback test sit in a settings sheet, opened from a quiet Audio & camera settings link. Start matching is the only primary CTA.
  • Flashcards while waiting are the Solo Room deck — the same flip card, bookmark, replay player and glass nav dock, in a Café waiting shell rather than a room.
  • Every decision surface is the Gym milky bottom sheet: match found, the agreement, editing levels mid-search, and Leave & report. The depleting .respline carries the response window, as it does for Gym consent.
  • Text is the Gym Main Room chat — overlay on the live video plus the glass composer (smiley, field, send). Compact and expanded match Gym. The Practice Room dock stays underneath.

Product decisions this pass encodes

  • Preferences are a continuous level range. All eligible levels are selected by default — the recommended matching state. The student may narrow or widen a single spectrum; there are no gaps, and their own level always stays inside the range. Level names and order come from LEVEL_DATA.eligible (placeholder data).
  • The helper is supportive, not a count. The default line is All levels selected — your best chance of meeting someone. A narrower range names the ends, and a very tight range adds that widening may help.
  • A/V check runs before matching, on every entry. Live mic feedback is the Gym echo on the Mic control. Device setup is behind a quiet settings affordance. The Entry CTA is a continuation, and Start matching lives at the end of the check.
  • Searching does not block. Matching is felt first. The first search line stays for the whole wait. Close (X) opens a keep-or-stop decision. Stop matching is never a silent exit. Flashcards are offered but never required, and a match interrupts them.
  • No self-view while searching. The camera is off in the queue — readiness was settled before it, and searching is not a pre-call lobby.
  • Match found interrupts. 30 seconds, real content — avatar, name, level, location, one onboarding-derived icebreaker. Accepting keeps the same surface and waits for the partner rather than navigating.
  • The agreement is not a checklist. Four principles to scan, then one personal acknowledgement that enables Café time! No per-item ticks, no decline — it only ever appears after both sides accepted.
  • Ending offers a new partner, or home. The primary action is Find me another partner; going home is a quiet text action.

Explicit placeholders

  • The Hub is not designed. The grey field on state 3b exists only so the matching indicator has something to sit over, and says so on screen.
  • Spin the Wheel and Challenge mode are entry points only. Tapping them acknowledges and stops — the experiences are a later pass. What this pass fixes is their place and weight in the dock.
  • Flashcards are a reference, not a system. Five placeholder cards, enough to show the interrupt behaviour.
  • The devices are mock hardware. Two options per input so “choose the right one” is a real interaction, not a settings surface.
  • Level names, the eligible set and the partner’s level come from the sampled palette, which is not authoritative product data.
  • Copy is provisional throughout.
